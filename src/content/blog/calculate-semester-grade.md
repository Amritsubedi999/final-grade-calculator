Your semester grade is determined by the grading system in your course. The first step is to identify whether the syllabus uses weighted categories, total points, or another method.

## For weighted categories

Multiply each category average by its share of the course, then add the results. For example, if tests are 50% with an 86% average, assignments are 30% with a 94% average, and a project is 20% with an 80% score:

`86 × 0.50 + 94 × 0.30 + 80 × 0.20 = 87.2%`

Check that the category weights add to 100% if they describe the full course. If some work remains, a portal’s current average may be based only on completed categories and therefore differ from the final semester grade.

## For total points

When every point counts equally, divide total points earned by total points possible and multiply by 100:

`Percentage = (points earned ÷ points possible) × 100`

For example, 428 points earned out of 500 possible is `428 ÷ 500 × 100 = 85.6%`. Include only the point totals your course policy counts. Extra credit or dropped assignments may change the denominator or total.

## Know what remains

To estimate a final grade before the term ends, include future work with its course weight or possible points. Do not treat a current average as though it already accounts for assessments that have not happened.

If a final exam is a separate course weight, add the current coursework contribution and the possible final-exam contribution. ## Verify the syllabus rules

Instructors may use curves, minimum exam requirements, rounding rules, dropped scores, or category reweighting. A basic calculation cannot know those rules unless you account for them yourself. Use the official syllabus or ask your instructor to verify a result that matters.
