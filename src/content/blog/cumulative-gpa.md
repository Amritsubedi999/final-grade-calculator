A cumulative GPA summarizes grade points across courses over time. At many institutions, courses contribute in proportion to their credit hours, so a four-credit course has more influence than a one-credit course. Exact rules and scales differ by institution.

## Credit-weighted GPA formula

For each course, multiply its grade-point value by its credits. Add those quality points, then divide by the total credits included:

`Cumulative GPA = total quality points ÷ total GPA credits`

Suppose you earned a 3.0 in a 3-credit course and a 4.0 in a 4-credit course:

- First course: `3.0 × 3 = 9` quality points
- Second course: `4.0 × 4 = 16` quality points
- Combined GPA: `(9 + 16) ÷ (3 + 4) = 3.57`

This example assumes both courses count toward GPA and use the same grade-point scale.

## Why your school’s rules matter

Institutions may treat repeated courses, withdrawals, pass/fail classes, transfer credits, and incomplete grades differently. Letter-to-point conversions also vary. A percentage does not map to a universal GPA; use the conversion and credit rules published by your school.

## Semester GPA versus cumulative GPA

A semester GPA uses courses from one term. A cumulative GPA combines the courses counted across multiple terms. To update a cumulative GPA, use the existing quality points and credits plus the new term’s quality points and credits, rather than taking the simple average of semester GPAs.
