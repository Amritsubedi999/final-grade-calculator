Knowing the score you need on a final is a weighted-average problem. You need three values: your current grade before the final, your target overall course grade, and the final exam’s share of the course grade.

## The required-final formula

Let **C** be your current coursework grade as a percentage, **T** your desired overall course grade, and **W** the final exam weight as a decimal. The coursework completed so far contributes `C × (1 − W)` to the final course grade. The exam must provide the rest:

`Required final score = (T − C × (1 − W)) ÷ W`

For example, if your current grade is 84%, the target is 88%, and the final is worth 30%, use `W = 0.30`:

`(88 − 84 × 0.70) ÷ 0.30 = 97.33%`

You would need about 97.34% to reach at least 88%, depending on your course’s rounding policy.

## Check whether the target is possible

A result above 100% means the target cannot be reached with a final capped at 100%, given those inputs. The highest possible course grade is:

`Maximum course grade = C × (1 − W) + 100 × W`

If the required score is 0% or less, your current coursework contribution already meets the target under this model. A zero-weight final cannot change the grade, so compare your current grade directly with the target instead of dividing by zero.

## Enter the course weight correctly

Use the final’s percentage of the **whole course grade**, not its share of the remaining coursework. If the syllabus says the final is 25% of the course, enter 25%. The coursework portion is then 75%.

The calculation assumes your current grade represents the average across all non-final coursework and that the final is the only remaining work. If other assignments remain, account for them using your syllabus or a weighted category planner.
